---
title: "Understanding Compound Interest"
category: Finance
difficulty: Beginner
---

Compound interest is often called the eighth wonder of the world. Unlike simple interest, which is calculated only on the original amount, compound interest is calculated on both the original amount and the interest already earned. Over time, this creates exponential growth.

Consider this example: investing $1,000 at a 7% annual return means you earn $70 in the first year. In the second year, you earn interest on $1,070, and so on. After 30 years, your initial investment could grow to more than $7,600.

The key insight is that time is your greatest ally in investing. Starting early — even with small amounts — can produce dramatically better outcomes than starting later with larger sums. This is why financial advisors emphasize the importance of beginning to save as soon as possible.

Key vocabulary: compound, interest, investment, exponential, return
