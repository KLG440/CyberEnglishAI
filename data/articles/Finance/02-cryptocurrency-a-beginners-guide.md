Cryptocurrency is digital money that uses cryptography to secure transactions and control the creation of new units. Bitcoin, the first cryptocurrency, was created in 2009 and remains the most well-known, but thousands of alternatives now exist.

Cryptocurrencies operate on blockchain technology — a distributed ledger maintained by a network of computers. This design means no single institution controls the currency, and transactions are recorded transparently and permanently.

Investing in cryptocurrency carries significant risks. Prices are extremely volatile, regulatory frameworks are still evolving, and security incidents have resulted in substantial losses. Financial experts generally recommend that cryptocurrency represent only a small portion of a diversified portfolio, and that investors never commit money they cannot afford to lose.

Key vocabulary: cryptocurrency, cryptography, blockchain, ledger, volatile
